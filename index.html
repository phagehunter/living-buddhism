Living Buddhism

A character map for Julia Cassaniti's ethnography of a Thai Buddhist community, by Curtis Hoffmann

This single-page dashboard is a study companion to Living Buddhism: Mind, Self, and Emotion in a Thai Community (Cornell University Press, 2015). It pairs distant reading, visualizations of the people, places and sections of the book, with close reading: scene-by-scene commentary, page citations and original section guides readable beside every view.

Move the section slider from the Prologue to the Conclusion and toggle the social circles (Gaew and Sen's family, Goy's household, Mae Jaeng town, Ban Ko Tao and visitors) to re-draw every view; a playback control animates the book section by section. Clicking a person, edge, path or waypoint opens the commentary panel with thematic tags from the book's own vocabulary (tham jai, jai yen, anicca, kreng jai), and each page citation links into the Guide tab.

What the three views show

  • Network Dynamics. The community of "Mae Jaeng": more than forty people across two families, the town's friends and healers, the Karen Christian village of Ban Ko Tao and the fieldwork circle. Node size follows how connected a person is; dashed edges mark the Prologue, which narrates the ending first.
  • Spatial Topography. The valley and its world: pseudonymised field sites placed only as far as the book's published distances and directions allow, documented places such as Chiang Mai, Bangkok and Lamphun, and paths for the ethnographer's widening circle, the ambulance to Chiang Mai and the family's devotional travel.
  • Narrative Gravity. Whose story each of the eight sections follows (Sen, Gaew, Goy, Ban Ko Tao and everyone else), with a flash-forward overlay on the Prologue.

The book is in copyright and is not reproduced here; names of people and places are the book's own pseudonyms, and the field-site pins are approximate by design.
